Daily mouth care between our visits.

The most important dental care in a community happens at the sink every day. Four things we teach every care team.

1

Brush twice a day, teeth or not

Soft brush, pea of fluoride toothpaste, gums and tongue included. Residents with no teeth still need their mouth cleaned.

2

Dentures out at night

Remove, brush with a denture brush, soak in water or cleanser. Never in a tissue, never on the tray. Labeled dentures come home.

3

Watch for dry mouth

Many medications reduce saliva, which speeds decay. Offer water often, use alcohol-free rinses and tell us when it starts.

4

Call when you see this

Swelling, bleeding that lasts, refusing food, holding the jaw, a sore that does not heal in two weeks. We would rather hear early.

Questions we hear from administrators and families.

Does on-site care satisfy our dental services requirement?
Federal rules require nursing facilities to provide or arrange routine and 24-hour emergency dental care, and to refer residents with lost or damaged dentures within three days. A written agreement with Arbor, with visit records and summaries on file, gives your policy a provider to name and your survey binder something to show.
What space do you need in our building?
A resident's room works for most care. For a full visit day, a quiet room with a chair, a table and an outlet is ideal. We do not need plumbing, a dental chair or any installed equipment.
How are families involved?
Responsible parties sign consent before the first visit and receive a written summary afterward. For anything beyond the routine exam and cleaning, we call with options and an estimate before treatment starts.
What does it cost the facility?
Care is billed to the resident's coverage or responsible party, not to the facility. We set out any program terms in the service agreement so there are no surprises in either direction.
Can you see residents with dementia or who resist care?
Yes. Visits are unhurried, in a familiar place, and often shorter and more frequent rather than long. Staff who know the resident are welcome to stay. We adapt what is done on a given day to what the resident can tolerate.
How fast can you respond to a lost or broken denture?
Call the same day. We will return the call within one business day and fit an assessment around our scheduled visit days. Repairs and relines are usually faster than a new denture, which takes several visits.
