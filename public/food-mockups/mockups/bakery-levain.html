(05) 48 hours

Why a loaf takes two days

“Most of the work is waiting. The flour and the levain do the rest.”

Hira Saeed started Levain & Co. from a home oven in 2019, feeding the same starter every morning. Today the bakery mills nothing and rushes nothing: whole-wheat chakki atta comes from a single farm in Okara, and every loaf spends a full night in the cold room before it meets the oven.

Hira Saeed · head baker
  1. HOUR 00 · DAY 1, 08:00

    Levain build

    A spoon of the 2019 starter is fed flour and water at 1:5:5 and left to rise and peak.

    26°C · 10 hours
  2. HOUR 10 · 18:00

    Autolyse

    Flour and water rest together for an hour so the bran softens and the gluten starts on its own.

    Hydration 78%
  3. HOUR 11 · 19:00

    Mix & salt

    The ripe levain and sea salt go in by hand. No commercial yeast, no improvers.

    2% salt · 20% levain
  4. HOUR 12–17

    Bulk ferment

    Four sets of coil folds in the first two hours, then the dough is left to grow by about half.

    25°C · 5 hours
  5. HOUR 17 · DAY 1, 01:00

    Divide & shape

    Each loaf is weighed, pre-shaped, rested, then shaped tight and set seam-up in a linen-lined banneton.

    900 g per country loaf
  6. HOUR 18–46

    Cold retard

    A long, cold night slows the yeast and lets the bacteria build the sour, nutty flavour and the blistered crust.

    4°C · 28 hours
  7. HOUR 46 · DAY 3, 06:00

    Score & bake

    Turned out cold, scored once at a low angle, baked with steam so the ear lifts.

    250°C then 230°C · 45 min
  8. HOUR 48 · 07:00

    On the counter

    Out of the oven, onto the racks, onto the shelf. Core temperature 97°C.

    Cool 1 hour before slicing
