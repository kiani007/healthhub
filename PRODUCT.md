## Platform

web

## Users

The primary audience is the owners and decision-makers of food businesses in Pakistan (cafés, restaurants and bakeries) being pitched a website. They judge each mockup by asking whether it would win customers for a business like theirs.

Each mockup also has its own end visitors: the customers of that fictional business in its city and neighbourhood (for example, coffee drinkers around F-7 Markaz, Islamabad, for Ember & Bean). A pitch only lands if the site plainly works for those customers.

## Product Purpose

The Tasting Board is a set of ten concept websites for fictional Pakistani food businesses: three cafés, three restaurants and four bakeries. A dashboard lets you browse them and preview each on desktop, tablet and phone. They exist to pitch web design to real food businesses.

Success means an owner who sees the mockup for their kind of business wants that site for themselves. A mockup succeeds when it would convert its own business's customers, not merely look good.

## Positioning

Each concept is built around one business's niche, city and the actions its customers actually take. There is a signature interaction specific to that business, and the details come from the business itself: menu, prices, hours and location. Maps and media are real, not drawn. A neighbouring template could not truthfully claim any of that specificity.

## Operating Context

- Shared as a live link (https://healthhub-sable.vercel.app/). The board is at `/` and each site is at `/mockups/<slug>`.
- Photos and video are AI-generated by the owner of this project from numbered prompts in `SHOT-LIST.md`. They are dropped into `public/assets/<slug>/` and wired through each page's `MEDIA` object. Until a slot has media, the page's illustrated fallback shows.
- Each page states that it is a concept for a fictional business ("concept, not a live site").

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Static HTML with no build step. There is one self-contained page per business in `public/mockups/`, and the board is `public/index.html`. It deploys on Vercel as plain static files (`vercel.json`).
- Libraries come from CDNs: GSAP with ScrollTrigger and SplitText, Lenis, and Leaflet with CARTO/OpenStreetMap tiles. Every page must still read and work if they fail to load.
- Every conversion flow is a working demo that never sends, books or charges anything. That covers order-ahead, subscriptions, bookings and newsletter forms, and each one says so in its confirmation.
- Per-business conversion goals live in `public/index.html` (the `MOCKUPS` data). Ember & Bean has three goals of equal weight: whole-bean subscriptions, café visits and order-ahead pickup.

## Brand Commitments

These rules are binding across every mockup:

- **Agency-grade.** The work must stand up as a premium, roughly $10,000 agency website. It must not read as a generic AI-generated or Claude-style template.
- **Real maps and media.** Locations use real interactive maps, never drawn ones. The owner's generated photos and video replace illustrated art wherever a shot exists.

## Evidence on Hand

- **Ember & Bean:** footage and photos are in `public/assets/cafe-ember-bean/`. That includes the hero pour video and its poster, plus six photos: cortado, roaster, bar, window, greens and cupping.
- **Aurelia:** the hero is a HeyGen clip.
- **Other mockups:** their shots (08–45 in `SHOT-LIST.md`) are not delivered yet, so those pages show illustrated fallbacks.
- **Do not fabricate:**
  - Every business, address detail, menu, price and opening hour is invented for the concept.
  - There are no real customers, reviews, testimonials, ratings, press or awards.
  - Future work must not add such proof as if it were real.

## Product Principles

1. **Sell to the owner by serving their customer.** Every mockup carries the real conversion paths of its kind of business: ordering, booking, subscribing, finding and calling. Decoration never stands in for them.
2. **One business, never a template.** Niche, neighbourhood, menu and signature interaction are specific enough that the site could not be reskinned for another business.
3. **Real over drawn.** Real maps always. Real media wherever it exists, with drawn art only as a fallback.
4. **Honest concepts.** Each mockup is clearly a concept for a fictional business. Demo flows never pretend to transact, and no proof is invented.
5. **Agency-grade finish.** Performance, accessibility and phone behaviour are part of the pitch, not polish for later.
